Alexa Shopping

2017–2018 · Amazon Alexa

I was the first product lead for how Alexa understood shopping requests. Alexa’s overall measures said shopping worked; I set out to test that. I listened to thousands of real requests, wrote how each would be judged, built the team that judged them, and ran the loop with Alexa’s scientists and engineers.

Amazon wanted Echo owners to shop by voice

In October 2017, Amazon said active Alexa customers had grown more than five times in a year.

The Echo Dot speaker launched at $49.99 in 2016. Amazon said it was the best-selling product on all of Amazon in the 2017 year-end shopping season. Amazon wanted Alexa customers to shop by voice. I was the first product lead for how Alexa understood a spoken shopping request.

Voice shopping at Amazon in 2017

Amazon’s annual Prime Day sale in 2017 had more than 100 deals available only through Alexa. In October 2017, The Times of Israel reported that Amazon planned voice shopping research centers in Tel Aviv and Haifa, with about 100 staff.

Ordering by voice, with no screen

A customer could say “Alexa, order” and a product such as paper towels, hear one offer with Prime delivery, and say “yes.”

An Echo speaker had no screen and no list of results. In Amazon’s cloud, speech recognition turned the sound into words, and language understanding models identified the product. Alexa offered an item from the customer’s order history first, then an Amazon’s Choice product, popular and well priced, then the top search result.

Paying by voice

In January 2017, The Register reported that a child’s request to an Echo had ordered a $160 dollhouse. Voice purchasing was on by default. Orders were charged to the payment method saved on the account, and a customer could require a four-digit code, spoken aloud.

Why a spoken shopping request was hard

Alexa’s overall quality measures said shopping was working well; we thought shopping was failing more often than those measures showed.

A spoken shopping request could fail in ways other requests did not. Alexa read out one product, so only that offer counted. Brand names were easy to mishear, and a speaker could not suggest completions the way a search box does. The same verb could mean different tasks; Amazon’s own 2018 example was “order me a cab” and “order me an Echo Dot.” A wrong order also cost the customer money.

Amazon’s public measure

In April 2018, Amazon reported that Alexa’s spoken language understanding had improved by more than 25% in 12 months. That figure covered all of Alexa in the United States, the United Kingdom and Germany.

Deciding how to judge a shopping request

My first job was to decide how a shopping request should be judged.

It was my first AI product role, so I started from customers’ real requests. Every week I listened to 1,000 sampled interactions and marked each turn, one request and Alexa’s reply, a success or a failure. From that listening I wrote the rubric, our written scoring guide: what counted as success for a shopping turn, with examples.

One request, judged by its offer

Under my rubric, a spoken shopping request succeeded only if Alexa offered the product the customer asked for.

One standard, applied by 10 analysts

I led a 10-person team of data analysts who applied the rubric to sampled shopping interactions.

Each type of error had a written definition and a set of examples, so that 10 people would label the same turn the same way. When the analysts found a case the rubric did not cover, we added it.

Why trained judges mattered

Agreement between judges shows whether a written guide is clear. On a five-level rating of each turn that Amazon published in 2019, expert judges agreed at 0.94 and novice judges at 0.75, on a measure where 1 is full agreement.

Measure, fix and measure again

I worked with Alexa’s language scientists and engineers in quick rounds; each failure went to the people who could fix it.

A request could break at any step: a misheard brand, the wrong task, the wrong product details or the wrong offer. Shopping success was the share of sampled spoken shopping requests that ended in the right offer, judged against my rubric. It rose from about 50% when I started to about 85% when I left.

Why one customer measure came first

Three steps that are each 90% accurate leave about one customer in four with an error. That is why one customer measure, shopping success, ranked above model measures such as speech recognition accuracy.

What was said about voice shopping

Amazon said millions of Amazon Prime members shopped by voice in the 2017 year-end shopping season.

The 2018 debate

In August 2018, the technology news site The Information reported that about 2% of Alexa owners bought by voice that year; Amazon disputed it. Reports named no screen and no reviews as reasons shoppers hesitated. In December 2018, Amazon said shopping use of Alexa had more than tripled from 2017.

From one spoken offer to Alexa for Shopping

In May 2026, Amazon combined its Rufus and Alexa+ assistants into a later product, Alexa for Shopping.

Other Amazon teams built it, eight years after my work on Alexa shopping. In 2017, one of Amazon’s Prime Day examples was “Alexa, order Greenies dog treats,” and the customer heard one offer. Alexa for Shopping works by voice or touch, in the Amazon Shopping app, on the website and on Echo Show devices. One of Amazon’s example requests for it is “add my regular dog treats.”
